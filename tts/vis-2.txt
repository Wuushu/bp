Stop two. Why build up here?

Catch your breath. You've earned it. And you might be wondering: why on earth would anyone build a castle this high up?

The answer is one terrifying event. In twelve forty-one, the Mongol army swept into Hungary. They were fast, they were organised, and they were devastating. Towns burned. Wooden walls and earth ramparts were useless against them. Large parts of the country were left in ruins, and the king, Béla the Fourth, barely escaped with his life.

When the Mongols suddenly turned around and left, Béla drew a lesson that changed the whole Hungarian landscape: build in stone, and build high. Castles started appearing on hilltops all over the country. And here at Visegrád, the king and his queen built two: one down by the river, and one up on the peak. The one you're climbing to now.

You might remember King Béla from Buda Castle. Same king. Same idea. Same fear.

And think about what this climb means for an attacker. Every soldier, every ladder, every heavy siege machine has to be dragged up this slope, while the defenders simply wait at the top. Now imagine doing it in armour.

So, keep going. The path gets steeper, but it's worth it. And when you reach the top, I have a story for you. About a crime.
