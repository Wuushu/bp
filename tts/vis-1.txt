Stop one. The Danube Bend.

Take a look at the river. For hundreds of kilometres, the Danube has been flowing east across Europe, minding its own business. And then, right here, it runs into a wall of hills, and is forced to swing south, towards Budapest. Those hills are what's left of ancient volcanoes, which erupted around fifteen million years ago. The river has been carving its way through them ever since. That's the Danube Bend.

Now, here's a fun fact about where you're standing. For about four hundred years, this river was the edge of the Roman Empire. The far bank, where you're heading, was Rome: roads, forts and soldiers. This bank, where you're standing right now, was what the Romans considered barbarian land. So, while you wait for the ferry, technically, you're a barbarian. Congratulations.

The Romans built a fort on a hill just north of Visegrád, to keep an eye on the river. After they left, Slavic-speaking people settled here and gave the place its name. Visegrád means something like "the high castle". And as you'll find out after about forty-five minutes of walking uphill, they meant "high" quite literally.

So, enjoy the crossing. In about ten minutes, you'll step into the Roman Empire, and then climb up to a castle that once guarded the most precious object in the kingdom.
