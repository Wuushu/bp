Did you know? Six fun facts about Visegrád.

One. Visegrád's old German name was Plintenburg. One theory links it to the word for "blinding". The view from up here was supposedly that dazzling.

Two. The Romans built a triangular fort on the hill just north of town, in the first half of the fourth century. Their soldiers left in the early fifth century, as the empire fell apart.

Three. Solomon's Tower, the big six-sided tower down by the river, is named after King Solomon of Hungary. Legend says he was held prisoner here in the eleventh century. The catch? The tower was only built about two hundred years later.

Four. Baby Ladislaus, crowned with the crown stolen from this castle, was just twelve weeks old at his coronation. He was born in February, fourteen forty, and crowned that May.

Five. The Holy Crown has had quite a journey since. At the end of the Second World War, it was carried out of Hungary to keep it from the Soviet army, and it spent much of the Cold War at Fort Knox, in the United States. It came home in nineteen seventy-eight, and since the first of January, two thousand, it has been on display in the Parliament in Budapest.

Six. Every July, Visegrád goes back to the Middle Ages. The International Palace Games bring more than two hundred knights and warriors to town, with tournaments by the castle, a medieval fair on the main street, and torchlit parades to the Royal Palace.

That's all six. Enjoy the view.
