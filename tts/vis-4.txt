Stop four. A summit with a view.

Find a spot on the walls, and look out over the river. This is the view everyone remembers: the Danube swinging around the hills, Nagymaros on the far bank, and the forests of the Börzsöny hills rising behind it.

In the thirteen twenties, King Charles the First of Hungary liked this place so much that he moved his royal court to Visegrád. And in thirteen thirty-five, he used it to host one of the great diplomatic meetings of the Middle Ages. He invited the King of Bohemia and the King of Poland, and they stayed for about two months. Between the feasts, they settled old quarrels, and agreed on new trade routes that let their merchants avoid Vienna, which forced every passing merchant to stop and sell there. Think of it as a medieval trade summit. With a better view.

Fast forward six and a half centuries. In February nineteen ninety-one, communism has just collapsed across Central Europe. The leaders of Hungary, Poland and Czechoslovakia need a place to meet and plan their future together. And they choose Visegrád, on purpose, as a nod to the meeting of thirteen thirty-five. That partnership still exists. It's called the Visegrád Group. So, when you hear about it on the news, this is the place it's named after.

Take your time up here. This is the highest point of the day. Lunch is about five minutes away, and from here on, it's all downhill.
